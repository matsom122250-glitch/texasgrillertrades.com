Project Star: a $22.3B Texas gas plant for AI data centers — and what it means for NextEra (NEE)

Research note · Published Oct 14, 2026 · NEE quote as of Oct 16, 2026

On Sept. 30, 2026, the U.S. Commerce Department and the Government of South Korea named NextEra Energy Resources and Related Companies, with Lewis Energy Group, to develop Project Star — a $22.3 billion, 6.47-gigawatt natural gas power campus in Encinal, Texas, built to feed a 5 GW AI data center campus next door. It is the first project funded under Korea's $200 billion U.S. investment pledge. Here is what was announced, and what it does and doesn't mean for NextEra stock.

Why it matters

It is "bring your own power" at hyperscale. Texas has pushed large data centers to bring their own generation instead of leaning on the ERCOT grid. Project Star does exactly that: the plant sits next to the load, and only surplus power flows to the grid. If it works, expect it to become a template for other AI campuses.

It is a real, named deal for NextEra's gas pivot. NextEra built its reputation as the largest renewable developer in the country. When it laid out a push into data centers and natural gas in December 2025, the stock dipped and analysts said they wanted announcements turned into signed projects. A government-backed, multi-gigawatt gas campus is that kind of proof point.

It is industrial policy, not just a power plant. The same announcement included up to $120 billion for eight U.S. nuclear reactors ("Project Power") and work on Alaska LNG ("Project North"). Energy for AI is now part of trade negotiations between allies.

What it means for NEE stock

The market shrugged. The news hit after the close on Sept. 30. NEE rose 0.8% the next day — about the same as the utilities sector — and is up 3.2% since, trailing the utilities ETF (+5.5%) and well behind merchant power names Vistra and Constellation, which each gained about 16% over the same stretch.

Why the muted reaction? Three reasons stand out:

  • NextEra doesn't own it. The two governments jointly own the energy campus; NextEra's joint venture builds and operates it. How much NextEra earns — development fees, construction margin, operating contracts or any equity stake — has not been disclosed. Until it is, investors can't put a number on it.
  • It is years away. First power is targeted for 2029 and full output for 2032. Nothing here changes 2026 or 2027 earnings. NextEra guides 2026 adjusted EPS to $3.92–$4.02 and targets roughly 8% annual growth through 2032; Project Star fits that long-range story more than it changes it.
  • Valuation already prices in growth. At $79.78, NEE trades at about 20.1× the midpoint of 2026 guidance and yields about 3.1% on its $2.49 annual dividend. That is a premium to the typical regulated utility.

The other side of the ledger: the stock is about 19% below its 52-week high of $98.75 and only a few dollars above its low of $74.41. If NextEra shows that it earns real money from Project Star — and that more deals like it are coming — that gap gives the stock room to re-rate.

What to watch next

  • Q3 earnings, Wed. Oct. 21 (before the open). The first chance to hear management explain NextEra's economics in Project Star and whether similar deals are in the pipeline. NextEra investor relations.
  • Equipment and construction. Large gas turbines have long lead times industry-wide. Which Korean suppliers are named, and on what schedule, will show whether 2029 is realistic.
  • The trade deal itself. Funding depends on Korea's $200 billion commitment, which is capped at $20 billion a year. Any friction in U.S.–Korea relations is a risk to the timeline.
  • Related Digital's tenants. The 5 GW data center campus is privately funded. Signed hyperscale tenants would de-risk the whole project.
  • Congress and insider trades in NEE. We track filings hourly.

Bottom line

Project Star is a big, visible win for NextEra's strategy of selling power directly to data centers, and it puts the company at the center of a U.S.–Korea energy partnership. But it is government-owned, a multi-year build-out, and its contribution to NextEra's earnings is still unknown — which is why the stock barely moved. The Oct. 21 earnings call is where that question starts to get answered.
