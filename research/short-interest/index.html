Most shorted stocks: short interest & days to cover

Research note · Updated Oct 12, 2026 · FINRA data as of Sep 30, 2026

Short interest measures how heavily investors are betting against a stock. When short interest as a percentage of shares outstanding climbs above 20–30%, it signals significant market skepticism — but also the potential for a short squeeze if sentiment shifts. What to watch: squeeze candidates

Two metrics together identify the highest short-squeeze risk: a high short % of float and a high days-to-cover number. That combination means many shares are shorted and it would take a long time for shorts to exit — a rising price can cascade quickly.

From this list, the names with both high short interest and high days to cover include:

  • LENZ (33.6% short, 13.8 days to cover) — LENZ Therapeutics, a clinical-stage ophthalmology company. High short interest likely tied to binary clinical outcome risk.
  • CCCC (32.8%, 17.7 days) — C4 Therapeutics, a targeted protein degradation biotech. 17.7 days to cover is the highest on this list.
  • JACK (33.1%, 12.1 days) — Jack in the Box, under pressure from fast-food industry headwinds and franchise restructuring.
  • WOLF (50.7%, 11.4 days) — Wolfspeed, a silicon carbide semiconductor maker struggling with overcapacity and demand shortfalls in EV and industrial markets.
  • HTZ (39.0%, 10.6 days) — Hertz, which emerged from bankruptcy in 2021 and has faced ongoing EV fleet write-down and profitability issues.

High short interest is not a buy signal by itself — it often reflects real fundamental problems. But it is a dynamic worth watching, especially when a catalyst (earnings beat, acquisition, FDA approval) hits a heavily shorted name.

Short interest glossary

TermWhat it means
Short interestThe total number of shares that have been sold short and not yet covered or closed out.
% of shares outstandingShort interest divided by total shares outstanding. A higher percentage means a larger fraction of the company is being bet against. Can exceed 100% because shares can be borrowed and reshorted.
Days to coverShort interest divided by average daily trading volume. Tells you how many days it would take all short sellers to buy back their positions at normal volume. Higher = harder to exit.
Short squeezeWhen a heavily shorted stock rises sharply, forcing short sellers to buy shares to close their positions, which drives the price even higher. High days-to-cover names are most vulnerable.

How FINRA reports short interest

FINRA collects short interest data from broker-dealers twice a month, with settlement dates around the 15th and the last business day of each month. Data is reported within a few days of each settlement. The figures here are from the Sep 30, 2026 settlement, published in early October 2026.

Note: FINRA short interest data covers U.S.-listed stocks and captures all reported short positions at the settlement date. It does not identify individual short sellers — only aggregate position sizes by ticker. For institutional-level short positioning (who is short and by how much), that data comes from prime brokers and is not publicly available for free.
