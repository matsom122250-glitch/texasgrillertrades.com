Who invests in Tiger Global, and what its TSM stake tells you

Research note · Updated Oct 2, 2026

Tiger Global Management is one of the world’s largest technology investors, and one of the biggest recent buyers of Taiwan Semiconductor (TSM). Its investor list is private, but public pension reports, foundation filings and press coverage name 17 outside investors, alongside the firm’s own partners and staff. Its long-run record is strong, its record since 2021 is not, and its TSM stake is one of several large technology bets.

Tiger Global and Taiwan Semiconductor

  • TSM was Tiger Global’s fourth-largest disclosed stock holding as of Q1 2026, at 8.2% of the portfolio, behind Alphabet, Nvidia and Amazon.
  • In Q2 2026 it added about 1.84 million shares, taking the stake to roughly 5.57 million shares worth about $1.88 billion.
  • It was not alone: 1,482 institutions added TSM shares in their most recent quarter and 1,228 cut their positions.

What the stake does and does not tell you

  • It is a broad technology bet. The same portfolio holds Alphabet, Nvidia, Amazon, Meta and Broadcom, as many large funds do.
  • The record cuts both ways. The same conviction in technology produced the 2020 gains and the 2022 loss.
  • The filings are late and partial. Quarterly 13F reports arrive up to 45 days after the quarter ends and show only U.S.-listed holdings, not hedges, short positions or sales made since.
  • A fund is not an individual. It can hold a large position inside a hedged portfolio and sell it in a day.

Tiger Global’s buying shows that one experienced technology investor likes TSM. The case for or against the stock rests on the company itself: its valuation, demand for AI chips, and the risks of manufacturing in Taiwan.

Limits of this research

  • The named investors are a small slice of the total. Wealthy individuals, family offices and most endowments disclose nothing.
  • The fund numbers shown for each investor are only the ones we could see; an investor may be in other funds too.
  • Fortune reported that CalPERS’s $300 million went into the 14th fund. CalPERS’s own records say PIP 15, which we use here.
  • University endowments are widely reported as investors, but we found none named.
